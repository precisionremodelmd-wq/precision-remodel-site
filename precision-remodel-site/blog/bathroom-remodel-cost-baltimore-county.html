What Drives the Cost of a Bathroom Remodel in Baltimore County

November 22, 2026  ·  8 min read

Precision Remodel's bathroom remodels start from $16k, and the final number depends on scope: a finish refresh, a full gut to the studs, or a layout change with moved plumbing. In Baltimore County, plumbing, shower waterproofing, tile, permits and fixture choices move the price most. No honest number exists until someone sees the room.

This guide is for homeowners in Towson and the surrounding northeast Baltimore County neighborhoods who are planning a high-end bathroom and want to understand the reasoning behind the price, not just a figure. Precision Remodel (MHIC #151439) is led by a working home inspector, so we plan every bathroom around what inspectors flag.

Bathroom remodel cost in Baltimore County: why it varies

Our bathroom remodels start from $16k. Beyond that starting point, cost depends on scope, and we won't quote a one-size-fits-all number because two bathrooms of the same size can differ by a wide margin.

The price is built from a handful of decisions:

  • How much you are taking out. Replacing a vanity and mirror is a different job from removing the tub, floor and walls.
  • Whether plumbing moves. Keeping fixtures where they are is the cheapest path. Relocating a toilet, shower or drain adds labor and, often, work below the floor.
  • What's behind the walls. Water damage, outdated supply lines or poor framing can't be priced until the wall is open. Older homes in the Towson area are more likely to hold surprises than newer ones.
  • How much tile and what kind. Square footage of tile, tile size and pattern all change labor.
  • Fixtures and finishes. A freestanding tub, a custom vanity or a linear drain cost more to buy and more to install than standard versions.
  • Permits and inspections. Fees and requirements are set by the county, so confirm them with Baltimore County.

The same logic holds across Maryland. Searches for bathroom remodel cost in Maryland or Baltimore will turn up broad averages, but your scope, your house and your finish level decide the real figure.

What are the scope tiers: refresh, full gut, or layout change?

Most bathroom projects fall into three tiers, and each tier adds cost for a clear reason. Knowing which one you're in is the single most useful step in budgeting.

TierWhat it usually includesWhy the cost rises
RefreshNew vanity, fixtures, lighting, paint, possibly flooringMostly materials and short labor; walls and plumbing stay
Full gutDemolition to the studs, new shower or tub, tile, waterproofing, vanity, fixturesEvery trade is involved; hidden conditions get exposed
Layout changeFull gut plus moved fixtures, relocated drains, new walls or framingPlumbing rerouting, floor and wall framing, added inspections

Our starting price of $16k reflects the lower end of this range. Where a specific project lands depends on scope and is set after we've seen the space.

A high-end bathroom usually lands in the full-gut or layout-change tier. If you want a larger shower, a double vanity or a relocated tub, plan for the work behind the finishes, not only the finishes themselves.

If a layout change involves cutting or altering floor joists or removing a wall, that's a structural question. We bring in a structural engineer for those decisions rather than guessing. Our guide to what makes a wall load-bearing explains why.

Where does the budget go: plumbing, waterproofing and tile?

In a full bathroom remodel, most of the budget goes to the parts you won't see after the job: plumbing, waterproofing and tile preparation. These are also what inspectors look at most closely.

Plumbing

Plumbing cost depends on how much of the system you touch. Swapping a toilet or faucet in place is simple. Moving a shower valve, adding a second sink or relocating a drain means opening walls and sometimes the floor, and the drain line has to keep its slope to work properly.

In older houses, supply or drain pipes may need replacement once the wall is open. We tell clients upfront that this is a possibility, because it can't be confirmed until demolition.

Waterproofing

A shower is a water-containment system, not just tile. The cost here is in the substrate, the membrane or waterproofing layer, the shower pan or curb, and the detail work at corners, niches and the drain. A cheap bid often reduces this line, and failures here show up years later as leaks or damaged framing below.

Tile

Tile cost has two parts: the material and the labor. Large-format tile, intricate patterns, mosaics, tiled niches and full-height walls all take more setting time and more precise prep. Flatter floors and walls make for better tile work, so some projects need leveling or reframing before tile goes on.

What do permits and inspections cost? Confirm with Baltimore County

Permit requirements and fees vary by scope and are set by Baltimore County, so we don't quote a figure here. Ask the county permits office, or ask your contractor to confirm what your specific project needs before you sign.

Generally, the more you change, the more likely a permit is involved: moved plumbing, new electrical circuits or altered framing are the usual triggers. A cosmetic refresh may need less. We handle the permit process on projects that require it, and we plan the schedule around inspection steps.

Because our owner works as an inspector, we build jobs to be inspection-ready: accessible work at rough-in, documented waterproofing steps and clean final details. A contractor who skips permits may quote lower, but unpermitted work can create trouble when you sell or file an insurance claim.

How do high-end finishes change the scope?

High-end finishes raise the price through materials and through the extra work they require. A premium fixture is often a bigger structural and plumbing commitment than it looks.

Examples of how a finish choice changes scope:

  • Freestanding tub: may need floor reinforcement review, a floor-mounted or wall-mounted filler, and a specific drain location.
  • Curbless or walk-in shower: requires the floor to be recessed or re-sloped, which can involve framing changes.
  • Heated floors: add electrical work, a dedicated circuit and a dedicated installation step before tile.
  • Custom vanity or built-ins: involve longer lead times and careful measurement; the plumbing rough-in must match the final cabinet.
  • Large-format or natural stone tile: needs flatter surfaces and more skilled setting, and some stone needs sealing.
  • Specialty fixtures and rain heads: may require upgraded valves, supply lines or extra framing for mounting.

Decide on finishes early. Changing a tub or a shower type after rough-in is one of the most common sources of added cost. For product lines and design options, see our bathroom remodeling page.

How do you read a bathroom remodel estimate?

A good estimate separates scope into clear lines so you can compare bids on the same basis. If two bids differ widely, the difference is usually in what's included, not in which contractor is more efficient.

Look for these items:

  1. Demolition and disposal: what's removed and who hauls it.
  2. Plumbing and electrical: whether they're included, and which fixtures are moving.
  3. Waterproofing method: what system is used in the shower and how it's detailed.
  4. Tile: square footage, tile type and who supplies it.
  5. Allowances: a placeholder for fixtures or tile you haven't chosen yet. A low allowance can make a bid look cheaper than it will end up.
  6. Permits: who pulls them and whether fees are included.
  7. Hidden conditions: how the contract handles rot, old pipes or framing problems found after demolition.
  8. License: Maryland home improvement contractors must hold an MHIC license. Ours is MHIC #151439.

Ask how change orders are priced and approved before work starts. Clear answers here prevent most budget disputes.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a bathroom remodel cost in Maryland?

It depends on scope. Precision Remodel's bathroom remodels start from $16k, and the total rises with demolition, plumbing changes, waterproofing, tile and fixture choices. Any firm figure needs an on-site look at your bathroom.

Why is a bathroom remodel hard to price over the phone?

The biggest costs are behind the finishes: pipes, framing and waterproofing. Those conditions often can't be judged without seeing the room, and older homes especially can hold surprises once the walls are open.

Does moving a toilet or shower raise the cost?

Usually yes. Relocating fixtures means rerouting drain and supply lines, and sometimes working in the floor framing. If framing is affected, we recommend a structural engineer's input.

Do I need a permit for a bathroom remodel in Baltimore County?

It depends on the work. Moved plumbing, new electrical circuits or framing changes commonly require permits, while a cosmetic refresh may not. Confirm with Baltimore County before starting.
