# Walk-In Shower vs Tub: What Adds More Resale Value in Maryland?

At some point in nearly every bathroom remodel conversation, the question comes up: should we replace the tub with a walk-in shower, or keep the tub? For homeowners in White Marsh, Perry Hall, and Essex who are planning a primary bathroom renovation and thinking about resale, the answer is more nuanced than most contractors will admit.

Here is an honest breakdown of what Maryland buyers actually expect, what consistently shows up in the data, and how to make the right decision for your specific home.

## What Maryland Buyers Actually Want

Buyer expectations in the Baltimore-area real estate market have shifted meaningfully over the past decade. Walk-in showers — particularly large-format tile showers with frameless glass enclosures — have become a near-universal expectation in primary bathrooms for homes priced above $400,000. For homes in the $550,000 to $850,000 range common in Cockeysville, Timonium, and established parts of Towson, a walk-in shower is increasingly considered standard rather than a distinguishing feature.

At the same time, buyers consistently expect at least one bathtub somewhere in the home. The concern is not that buyers necessarily intend to use a tub regularly — it is that eliminating every tub signals a limitation in resale flexibility, particularly for buyers with young children or those who simply prefer having the option.

The practical conclusion: a primary bathroom that converts a tub to a walk-in shower almost always reads as an upgrade in this market — *provided there is still a tub somewhere else in the home.*

## The Case for a Walk-In Shower

Walk-in showers outperform tubs on several dimensions that matter for resale.

**They signal a quality renovation.** A frameless glass enclosure with large-format tile, a rainfall showerhead, and a built-in bench reads immediately as a serious remodel. Buyers touring homes recognize the difference between a custom tile shower and a prefab insert, and they price it accordingly in their offers.

**They maximize usable space.** A standard 60-inch tub occupies roughly 22 to 25 square feet of floor space including clearance. A walk-in shower can occupy less than half that footprint — or fill the same area with a vastly more functional configuration. In primary bathrooms under 80 square feet, which are common in White Marsh homes built in the 1980s, the spatial gain is substantial.

**They are easier to maintain over time.** Large-format tile with minimal grout lines, frameless glass without a curb to scrub, and a linear drain are all meaningful maintenance advantages over a fiberglass tub surround. Buyers who have maintained older bathrooms understand this and factor it into their decisions.

For a primary bathroom remodel in northeast Baltimore County, converting a tub to a custom walk-in shower typically adds $8,000 to $20,000 to the project cost depending on tile selection, glass system, and shower hardware. Real estate professionals in this market consistently report that quality walk-in showers recover their cost reliably in homes at the right price point.

## The Case for Keeping a Tub

There are clear situations where keeping the tub makes more sense.

**If it is the only tub in the home.** Removing the last bathtub from a home is the one scenario where Maryland real estate professionals consistently advise against a shower conversion. A meaningful segment of buyers — families with young children, buyers thinking about resale flexibility, buyers who simply want the option — will remove a home from consideration if there is no tub anywhere on the property. This is a documented and tangible risk, not a theoretical one.

**If the bathroom is a secondary or shared space.** In a hall bath that serves multiple bedrooms, particularly in a home where the primary bathroom still has a tub, keeping the existing tub and upgrading the surround and fixtures is often the better use of the renovation budget. The cost of a full tub-to-shower conversion in a secondary bath may not translate to proportional resale value.

**If the home's price point does not support a premium shower.** Walk-in showers add resale value most reliably in homes where buyers expect them. In starter homes or entry-level townhouses, the premium for a custom tile shower may not translate to a meaningfully higher sale price.

## The Approach That Works Best

The optimal configuration for most northeast Baltimore County homes with more than one full bathroom is straightforward: convert the primary bathroom tub to a walk-in shower, and keep or upgrade the tub in the secondary bathroom.

This approach satisfies buyer expectations at both ends of the decision. The primary bathroom signals a quality remodel and modern living. The secondary bathroom preserves the tub that buyers with specific needs expect to find somewhere in the home. The property does not lose a market segment, and it gains a selling point in the room buyers evaluate most carefully.

Homeowners in White Marsh, Essex, and Perry Hall who follow this approach consistently find the combination easier to sell than either extreme.

## What Actually Determines Value: Quality of Execution

One point that often gets lost in the shower-versus-tub debate is that the quality of the installation matters more than which fixture you choose.

A walk-in shower with poor tile work, visible caulk failures at the glass, and a hollow-sounding floor can be worse for resale than a well-maintained original tub. A beautifully executed tile shower with quality frameless glass and a premium rain head will be a selling point in almost any market. Buyers can identify quality. They can also identify a renovation that ran out of money before the finish work was complete.

If budget is a constraint, a well-executed mid-range walk-in shower will consistently outperform an overly ambitious design that compromised on materials or workmanship to hit a price target. Do fewer things at a higher standard rather than more things at a lower one.
