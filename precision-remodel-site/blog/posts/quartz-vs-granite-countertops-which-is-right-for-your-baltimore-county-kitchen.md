For most Baltimore County kitchens, quartz is the easier choice. It is non-porous, never needs sealing, and the pattern is consistent from slab to slab. Granite is the better pick if you want a one-of-a-kind natural stone look or often set hot pans on the counter. Both hold up well; cooking habits and the look you want decide it.

## What is the real difference between quartz and granite?

**Granite is quarried natural stone. Quartz is an engineered product made mostly of ground quartz bound with resin.** That one fact explains almost every difference in care, appearance, and behavior.

- **Granite** varies slab to slab. Veining, color, and mineral flecks are all natural, so the slab you pick in the yard is the slab you get. It is porous to varying degrees depending on the stone, so it is usually sealed.
- **Quartz** is manufactured to a pattern, so samples look like the finished counter. It is non-porous, so it does not need sealing.

Neither is a bad choice. We steer homeowners based on how they live in the kitchen, not on which material is trending.

## Which is easier to maintain?

**Quartz is easier day to day, because there is no sealing and it resists staining.** Granite is not hard to live with, but it asks for a little more attention.

With granite, a sealer fills the pores in the stone. How often it needs resealing depends on the specific stone and the sealer, so ask the fabricator and follow what they tell you. Darker, denser granites tend to be less thirsty than lighter, more open ones, but ask your fabricator rather than assume.

With quartz, regular cleaning is about it. Skip harsh chemicals and abrasive pads, and follow the manufacturer's care guide, since brands differ.

If you are a busy household in Perry Hall or White Marsh with kids, red wine, and turmeric in constant rotation, the lower-maintenance surface often wins.

## Which handles heat better?

**Granite tolerates heat better. Quartz can be damaged by a hot pan because the resin can scorch or discolor.** This is the most overlooked difference when people shop from showroom samples.

Granite is formed under extreme heat, so a hot pot is less likely to hurt it. Even so, we recommend trivets on any stone, because sudden temperature changes can stress a slab and heat can harm the sealer.

Quartz manufacturers generally tell you to use trivets always. If you pull pans off the burner and set them down without thinking, be honest with yourself about that habit before choosing quartz. Also keep in mind that most quartz is not recommended for outdoor kitchens, since sun exposure can affect the color. Ask the manufacturer if you are planning anything outside.

## How do looks and resale compare in Baltimore County?

**Both can look high-end. Choose based on the look you want and what is normal for your neighborhood, not on a promised resale bump.** We can't promise a return on any material, and nobody honestly can.

Granite suits homeowners who like movement, depth, and natural variation. Quartz suits those who want a clean, uniform look, from soft whites to bold solids, with predictable results. Many quartz patterns imitate marble without marble's tendency to etch and stain.

In communities like Perry Hall, White Marsh, Parkville, and Essex, homes tend to be comparable to their neighbors. A finish level that matches the quality of the house and the cabinets reads as intentional to buyers. Going far above the neighborhood rarely pays back in proportion. ## What drives countertop cost more than the material name?

**Slab grade, edge profile, layout, and fabrication usually move the price more than the word quartz or granite.** The two materials overlap widely in price, so we can't tell you one is simply cheaper. Pricing depends on scope, and a fabricator's quote for your exact layout is the only number worth trusting.

These are the things that change a countertop quote:

1. **Slab choice.** Within each material there are entry-level and premium slabs. A rare granite can cost more than a mid-range quartz, and the reverse is also true.
2. **Square footage and layout.** A galley kitchen and a kitchen with a large island use slabs very differently. Waste from cutting around the pattern matters too.
3. **Seams.** Long runs and L-shapes need seams. Where they go affects how the finished counter looks.
4. **Edge profile.** A simple eased edge costs less labor than an elaborate one.
5. **Cutouts.** Undermount sink, cooktop, faucet holes, and any special features add fabrication work.
6. **Backsplash and waterfall panels.** A full-height stone backsplash or a waterfall island end is a different quantity of material and labor than a tile backsplash.
7. **Removal and disposal.** Taking out the old tops, and what is under them, is part of the job.

When you compare bids, make sure each one lists the same slab, edge, sink cutout, and seam plan. Our [line-by-line estimate checklist](/blog/compare-remodeling-estimates-maryland) shows how to spot a bid that left something out.

## What do homeowners miss when picking a countertop?

**The countertop is not a stand-alone decision. It depends on the cabinets, the plumbing, the electrical layout, and the order of work.** This is where our background as inspectors shapes how we plan. We know what inspectors flag, so we think about those details before the stone is cut.

- **Cabinets must be solid and level first.** Stone is heavy, and the template is taken from installed cabinets. If you are choosing between [custom and stock cabinets](/blog/custom-cabinetry-vs-stock-cabinets-what-perry-hall-homeowners-need-to-know), make sure the boxes are installed properly and level before anyone measures for stone.
- **Template after cabinets, not before.** The fabricator measures once the cabinets are in. Changing a sink, cooktop, or appliance after templating can mean a remake. Lead time from template to install varies by fabricator, so ask them early. Our [week-by-week kitchen guide](/blog/what-to-expect-during-a-kitchen-remodel-week-by-week-in-baltimore-county) shows where templating fits in the schedule.
- **Plan outlets before the stone goes down.** Counter receptacles and their protection are part of the electrical inspection. It is far easier to place them correctly while the walls are open than to fix them afterward. Your electrician and the Baltimore County permits office are who confirm what applies to your job.
- **Dishwasher mounting.** You can't screw into stone, so the dishwasher is normally anchored to the cabinet sides. We confirm this with the appliance and the counter material up front.
- **Overhangs and seating.** How far a counter can overhang without extra support depends on the material, thickness, and the fabricator's requirements. Ask your fabricator. If a design calls for an unusual span, or you are removing a wall to open the kitchen, that is a question for a structural engineer, not a guess. See our guide to [removing a load-bearing wall](/blog/removing-a-load-bearing-wall-cost-permits-process).
- **See the actual slab.** Go to the stone yard. A small sample does not show veining, color shifts, or how a seam will land on your island.

## So which should you choose?

**Choose quartz if you want low maintenance and a consistent look. Choose granite if you want natural character and better heat tolerance.**

Quartz tends to fit best when:

- You want no sealing and minimal upkeep.
- You prefer a uniform or marble-look pattern.
- You have a busy household where spills sit for a while.

Granite tends to fit best when:

- You like natural variation and want something nobody else has.
- You cook a lot and want more forgiveness with hot cookware.
- You don't mind occasional sealing.

If you are still torn, bring both samples home, set them against your cabinet and flooring colors in your own kitchen light, and ask us or your fabricator about the specific slabs you like.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is quartz or granite more expensive?

There is no fixed answer. Both come in a wide range, and slab grade, edge, layout, and fabrication affect the total as much as the material. Get a written quote for your layout and slab.

### Can I put a hot pan on a quartz countertop?

It's best not to. The resin can scorch or discolor from direct heat. Use a trivet, and follow your manufacturer's care guide.

### Does granite stain?

It can if it isn't properly sealed, particularly lighter or more porous stones. A good sealer and prompt cleanup prevent most problems. Your fabricator can tell you how often your stone needs resealing.

### Does quartz or granite add more resale value?

Neither guarantees a return. Buyers respond to a kitchen that is well built and fits the neighborhood. A clean installation and good layout matter more than the label on the stone.

### Do I need a permit to replace countertops?

Swapping countertops alone often doesn't trigger the same permits as plumbing, electrical, or structural work, but it depends on what else is changing. Confirm with the Baltimore County permits office for your specific scope.
